Constabulary Record

What the city wrote down about you. Name, parentage, address, declared occupation, known associates — and the gaps, which sometimes say more than the filled fields. An archive is not the truth of a person. It is what the institution managed to establish, in the order it suited the institution to establish it.

Out-of-fiction note. This is a player page for the Mistborn Gilded Steps campaign. The files below describe fictional characters and are written in the voice of a fictional police force that errs, omits and classifies. Fields marked no record have not been filled in by the table yet; fields marked sealed exist and are not public.

Appendix — statement in the subject's own hand

I was a constable eleven years, in Knightbridge, in the division that looks after the part of the city I was born in. I know how a patrol is arranged, how a piece of evidence gets lost, and which drawer a case goes to die in without anyone having to give the order. I learned that by working, not by informing.

I live on the same row I always did. I watch the contracts arrive house by house, I watch the offer climb when a neighbour refuses, I watch who disappears from the street and who turns up measuring the pavement. It is no crime at all, they say. That is exactly why it works.

What I want is easy to say and hard to do: that the people who profit off others answer for it. The trouble is that when I am certain, I stop waiting for the proof and go looking for the one that agrees with me. I did that once. It cost me everything. I cannot promise I would not do it again tomorrow.

He is built to find things and to be right about them. Awareness and Speed are his high attributes, and his trained skills are the three that follow from that: Perception, Light Weapons and Allomancy. Tin is the seeing metal — a Tineye burns it and every sense opens at once — and his is nascent, which in this system means he has Snapped but has not been trained: the power arrives in flickers he does not fully command. His Hunter talent, Seek Quarry, lets him fix on one person and follow them; the rest of the file is about what he does when he has fixed on the wrong one.

He carries a hunting rifle, a breastplate, a uniform he is no longer entitled to, and a set of manacles. His Presence is 0. He cannot talk his way past anything — not a doorman, not a magistrate, not a friend. Everything he gets, he gets by being already certain and already there.

Open goal: train the Tin — the reward is the full power.

Appendix — statement in the subject's own hand

The woman who signs for the Synod is my grandmother, and she raised me. My mother brought us, me and Lhazweth, and left. I do not know where, I do not know why, and I no longer remember her face properly. Of my father I do not even know the name. You wrote "no record" three times on my file. I have lived with that all my life; for you it is only the form.

I learned Terris, I learned the scriptures, I learned to sit still. I was never good at any of the three. What I actually read were the Allomancer Jak stories, hidden inside the science books. At twelve I started going down to the harbour, and the harbour taught me faster: who pays, who collects, who delivers, and what it is worth to watch a corner for a whole night.

About the line that says "presumed": you presume because I am Terris. That is how this file was made, and it is how you make all of them. What I want is a good deal simpler than that. I want to put together enough to take a train to the Roughs and not come back. Out there, from what I am told, a man can still be something he chose himself.

Appendix — statement in the subject's own hand

My father was a bookkeeper. He spent thirty-one years adding up the money of the steel barons of Elendel and died owing three of them. Nothing was stolen from him: it was all written down, in small print, and he signed it. That is where I learned the only lesson that ever served me — money does not belong to the man who does the work, it belongs to the man who tells the story about the work.

I came to Bilming because Elendel has too many people who look closely. It is better here. The men who built this city have more money than they know how to spend and less lineage than they would like. They do not want paintings: they want someone from the capital to say, aloud and in front of witnesses, that they have taste. That I sell. And it is expensive.

You will notice the file above is immaculate, and entirely wrong. Do not correct it. I do not do this because of my father, and I do not do it to put anything right — I do it because it is good, and because the people who pay me deserve to lose every coin they display. If anyone at this table thinks that makes me better than the magnates, be assured I do not think so.

How to read this archive

The Constabulary records what it established, not what is true. A blank field may mean that nobody looked, that somebody preferred not to write it down, or that the information went into an annexe this archive does not open.

The appendix in the subject's own hand is the only part of a file that did not pass through the police. Where the two contradict each other, the contradiction is the point — not an error to be corrected.

Each portrait sits on a coloured field, and the colour is a metal. The metal is one the subject has a relation to — what kind of relation is the subject's business, and in more than one case the Constabulary does not know there is one. The rings belong to the archive; the fields do not.

The block at the foot of each file, marked character sheet, is not the Constabulary speaking. It is the table: what the character is in the rules, taken from the campaign's own sheets. Where it contradicts the file above it, the file above it is only what the city managed to write down.

Nothing here goes past the campaign's spoiler line, and no file reveals GM material. What a character hides from the others stays hidden.
