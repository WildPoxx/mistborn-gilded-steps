The city climbs in steps. Below are the quays, the warehouses, the rail yards, the workshops
and the houses held on old rents. Above are the clubs, the offices, and the windows that look
down at the harbour the way a man looks at an inventory. Industrialisation did not unseat
Scadrial's aristocracy: it bought shares.

## Public premise

Bilming prospers and does not share. New cranes, new rail, new façades — and on the same block,
contracts that shorten, rents that climb, and trades that vanish without notice.

**Yomend Steps**, called simply *The Steps* by the people who live there, is the low strip of
the waterfront. Dockhands, railway workers, workshops, boarding houses, gambling rooms, small
smuggling, and families who have been there for generations. Above the slope, the high steps
watch — and decide.

The characters do not begin as heroes. They begin as people with an address, a trade, a debt
and an attachment, in a district that has become interesting to people with capital.

## Spoiler line

This campaign opens **after the first two novels of the Second Era**, and works from that line.
Nothing here goes past it. If you have read further, keep out of play what the characters would
have no way of knowing.

Characters from the novels do not appear. What happens in the rest of Scadrial reaches Bilming
as news, rumour, a shift in the market, or distant political pressure.
